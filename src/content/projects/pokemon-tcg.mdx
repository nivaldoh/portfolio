Pokémon TCG is a card game with hidden information and long chains of small
decisions: which card to play, which attack to use, which cards to choose for an
effect. In these experiments the learner makes about 70 recorded decisions per
game. Unlike Metamon's battles, which it learns from a large archive of human
games, this Kaggle competition came with no large training dataset. I built a
workflow around the supplied game simulator to train policies by imitating
scripted players and by reinforcement learning, then to evaluate them against
fixed scripted opponents. The most useful result came from asking what
a strong-looking number actually meant.

## What I built

- A Gymnasium environment around the simulator. Each decision becomes a choice
  among legal options with a mask, the opponent's turns run inside the
  environment, and the reward arrives only when the game ends.
- Multi-card selections split into a sequence of masked picks closed by a STOP
  action, so training and evaluation handle them the same way.
- A PyTorch policy that embeds card and attack identities and scores every
  legal option against the board, the hand, the open menu and the cards already
  picked.
- Behaviour cloning from games between four imported scripted teachers, with
  whole games held out for validation. The cloned weights then move into a
  custom Stable-Baselines3 policy for PPO fine-tuning against mixtures of
  opponents.
- Seat-swapped evaluation of each deck against each scripted opponent, and a
  recorder that saves every decision of a game for inspection.

<Details summary="Environment and action handling">

- The environment wraps the battle loop in `reset()` and `step()`. It plays the
  opponent's turns itself and resolves decisions the learner does not make.
- Options are padded to 128 slots. The legal-action mask is exposed both in the
  observation and through `action_masks()`, the interface MaskablePPO expects.
- A multi-select decision becomes a series of masked single picks. A reserved
  STOP slot commits the chosen set, and the selection commits automatically
  once it reaches the maximum count.
- The reward is +1 for a win, −1 for a loss and 0 for a draw, given only at the
  end of the game.

</Details>

<Details summary="Policy network">

- The core network is plain PyTorch, independent of Stable-Baselines3. It keeps
  one learned embedding per card identity (32 dimensions) and per attack
  identity (16).
- Each option becomes a latent vector. The context combines the game state,
  embeddings for the 12 board slots, pooled hand and discard embeddings, and
  averages over the menu options and the options already picked, so each pick
  of a multi-select is conditioned on the earlier ones.
- One shared scorer turns every option into a logit; a separate head estimates
  the value of the position.

</Details>

## Learning from four scripted teachers

The first question was how much of the scripted players' behaviour the policy
could learn. Each model played mirror matches, where both sides use the same
deck, against each of the four teachers.

<ResultsChart variant="multi-deck" />

PPO alone reached 13.9%, close to the earlier baseline of about 13%. Behaviour
cloning reached 20.0%; tuning PPO after cloning reached 24.0%. The useful change
was in the training recipe, while the custom architecture alone showed little
improvement in this comparison.

Every model remained below parity with the teachers. The clone matched the
teacher's move on 79.6% of held-out decisions, leaving room for errors to
accumulate over a game. These are findings about this setup, not a general
verdict on the architecture or PPO.

<Details summary="Cloning data, transfer and PPO settings">

- The cloning data came from 2,000 games between the teachers, covering all 16
  ordered deck pairings with seats swapped: 286,035 recorded decisions. Games
  were split into training and holdout sets by game, so no game appears in
  both.
- Holdout accuracy was 0.796 (0.803 for single picks, 0.765 for multi-select),
  against 0.217 for choosing a random legal option.
- After the weights move into the Stable-Baselines3 policy, a check confirms the
  transferred policy produces the same logits before training continues.
- In the first fine-tuning recipe (learning rate 5e-5, target KL 0.02), the
  entropy bonus pushed the sharply peaked clone toward uniform choices.
  The recipe that worked used no entropy bonus, a learning rate of 3e-5 and a
  target KL of 0.01. It climbed from 17% to 24% over 153,600 steps and then
  plateaued. The Dragapult ex mirror rose from 7.0% after cloning to 22.5%
  (95% interval 17.3–28.8), the first per-deck gain from PPO in these
  experiments that was separated from the clone by its interval.
- The 9 July entries were evaluated over 200 seat-swapped games per deck and
  opponent, with the policy sampling its actions; the pooled figure combines
  the four mirror pairings.

</Details>

## One opponent, one strong number

Next I narrowed the task to Lucario against the scripted Lucario teacher.
A specialist clone followed by PPO reached a reported 82.6% over 800 games.
The teacher's games against itself were balanced, making 50% a useful reference
for this matchup.

<ResultsChart variant="specialist" />

## What did the improvement mean?

I recorded a separate 100-game audit to examine how the games ended. All 82
model wins came from the opponent running out of cards to draw. Deck-out is a
legal win condition; the finding points to exploiting this fixed opponent's
behaviour, rather than a scoring bug.

The campaign did not test transfer to other opponents. The high win rate alone
did not establish broader playing strength; inspecting the outcomes exposed
what the aggregate number left out.

<Details summary="The specialist's training stages">

| Stage (Lucario mirror only)                          | Win rate              | Games |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ----- |
| Clone trained on all four decks                      | 22.8%                 | 400   |
| Lucario-only clone (4,000 games, 537,922 decisions)  | 38.8%                 | 400   |
| Plus PPO, cautious recipe                            | plateau at 45.9–46.8% | 400   |
| Plus PPO, learning rate 1e-4 and target KL 0.02      | 84.0%                 | 200   |
| Final checkpoint                                     | 82.6% (79.8–85.1)     | 800   |

The Lucario-only clone matched the teacher's choice 0.934 of the time on
held-out games. Choosing the most likely action instead of sampling gave 85.0%
(95% interval 79.4–89.3) over 200 games. No evaluation game in this campaign
ended in a draw.

</Details>
